The plan

Tuesday is a normal work day; the Virginia households drive down Tuesday evening or Wednesday morning. Thanksgiving is a late lunch around 1 on Thursday. The drive from Northern Virginia routes, timing, stops
DistanceAbout 395 miles from Vienna, 375 from Manassas Time6 to 6½ hours without stops; allow 7½ with the kids RoutesI-95 → I-85 (Richmond, Durham, Greensboro): usually fastest, worst traffic. US-29 → I-85 (Warrenton, Charlottesville, Lynchburg, Danville, then I-85 at Greensboro): 20–30 minutes longer, four-lane most of the way with some stoplights through towns, and it avoids I-95 entirely; from Manassas it is the natural choice. Check both before leaving: live traffic. TuesdayNobody is taking the day off, so this is an after-work drive. The Tuesday before Thanksgiving is one of the heaviest driving days of the year and 3–7 PM is the worst of it; leaving after 7 PM puts you in Charlotte around 1–2 AM with sleeping kids. Wednesday morning (out by 6) is the other option and arrives in time for the parade. SaturdayThe Reids are leaving Saturday afternoon, after lunch, which puts them through Richmond in the early evening; it is slower than a morning departure but Saturday is still far better than Sunday. Garcias to confirm their plan.
Stops
  • I-95/I-85: rest area near Petersburg at 2 hours; South Hill, VA (exit 12) at 3 hours for gas and food; Durham at 4 hours if you want a real stop, the Museum of Life & Science is right off the highway.
  • US-29: Charlottesville at 2 hours (Bodo’s Bagels, UVA Lawn for a run-around); Lynchburg at 3 hours; Danville at 4½ hours is the last stop before I-85.

Where we stay

Three ways to run the week. Every version starts and ends in Charlotte.

Option 1

Charlotte all week

Cindy & Randy’s house on Nolen Lane is home base. Ryan and Kristi stay at their own place.
CostGroceries and a night or two outDrivingNone once we arriveGood forThe kids’ routine, a real kitchen, no packing twice
  • Wednesday-night parade uptown, Turkey Trot Thursday, Speedway Christmas any night
  • Everyone has a bed, a yard and a familiar kitchen
  • Nothing to book
Option 2

A mountain house

Asheville, Hendersonville, Lake Lure or the High Country. 1½ to 2½ hours from Charlotte.
Cost$1,600–3,900 for four nights on the shortlist, split four waysDriving1½–2½ hours each wayGood forFireplace and hot tub weather, everyone under one roof
  • Biltmore and Tweetsie are already decorated for Christmas
  • Highs in the 50s, lows in the 30s; a chance of snow at Beech Mountain
  • Cell service is thin at a lot of these
Option 3

A beach house

Ocean Isle, Carolina Beach, Pawleys or the Charleston islands. 3½ to 4 hours from Charlotte.
Cost$2,400–5,600 for four nights on the shortlist, split four waysDriving3½–4 hours each wayGood forMid-60s and sunny, empty beaches, off-season prices
  • Brookgreen Gardens’ Nights of a Thousand Candles starts Friday; Enchanted Airlie in Wilmington too
  • Water is 60° so the pools are for looking at
  • Longest drive, and most beach towns are quiet in November
A split week is also on the ballot: Thanksgiving lunch in Charlotte on Thursday, then Friday and Saturday nights at a house. It is the shortest booking and the most driving.
