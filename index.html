The plan

Tuesday is a normal work day; the Virginia households drive down Tuesday evening or Wednesday morning. Everyone sleeps at Cindy and Randy’s on Nolen Lane except Ryan and Kristi, who are twenty minutes away at their own place. Thanksgiving is a late lunch around 1 on Thursday. What is left to decide (how we do the meal, which outings, who arrives when) is on the ballot, which closes Wednesday, November 4.

The drive from Northern Virginia routes, timing, stops
DistanceAbout 395 miles from Vienna, 375 from Manassas Time6 to 6½ hours without stops; allow 7½ with the kids RoutesI-95 → I-85 (Richmond, Durham, Greensboro): usually fastest, worst traffic. US-29 → I-85 (Warrenton, Charlottesville, Lynchburg, Danville, then I-85 at Greensboro): 20–30 minutes longer, four-lane most of the way with some stoplights through towns, and it avoids I-95 entirely; from Manassas it is the natural choice. Check both before leaving: live traffic. TuesdayNobody is taking the day off, so this is an after-work drive. The Tuesday before Thanksgiving is one of the heaviest driving days of the year and 3–7 PM is the worst of it; leaving after 7 PM puts you in Charlotte around 1–2 AM with sleeping kids. Wednesday morning (out by 6) is the other option and arrives in time for the parade. SaturdayThe Reids are leaving Saturday afternoon, after lunch, which puts them through Richmond in the early evening; it is slower than a morning departure but Saturday is still far better than Sunday. Garcias to confirm their plan.
Stops
  • I-95/I-85: rest area near Petersburg at 2 hours; South Hill, VA (exit 12) at 3 hours for gas and food; Durham at 4 hours if you want a real stop, the Museum of Life & Science is right off the highway.
  • US-29: Charlottesville at 2 hours (Bodo’s Bagels, UVA Lawn for a run-around); Lynchburg at 3 hours; Danville at 4½ hours is the last stop before I-85.
